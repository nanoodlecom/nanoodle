What you have is a real polygon mesh of the dragon figurine: 30,000 triangles with UVs, delivered as one GLB with the textures embedded. The textures are 2048px base-colour and normal maps plus metallic-roughness maps, so the antiqued gold-bronze metal and dark patina read correctly under PBR lighting. These are surfaces you can light, edit and print — the thing a Gaussian splat is not.

The GLB imports directly into Blender, Unity, Unreal and Godot, and slicers such as PrusaSlicer, Bambu Studio and Cura accept GLB for 3D printing. The model arrives unrigged: character rigging is available as an option, but this workflow leaves it off.

The honest edges: Idol invents every side the photo did not show, following the survey's hidden-sides line — the dorsal neck and spine, the rear wing surfaces and the underside of the base. Fine detail lives in the texture maps, not the geometry; 30,000 triangles is a budget, so the slender whiskers, thin horns, dorsal spines and wing membranes are approximate. Thin, shiny or transparent parts are approximate too. The mesh holds one object only, and its scale is relative, not measured.
