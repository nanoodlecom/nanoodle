**What You Got**
You receive a layer stack. The stack contains a clean base plate with the stone bust and pedestal removed and the area inpainted. The background is a separate transparent PNG. The main subject, the weathered stone bust, is its own named layer. The supporting element, the stone pedestal base, is also its own named layer. Stacking these layers at their provided coordinates reconstructs the original image.

**What to Do With Them**
Use these layers for precise compositing. A direct application is to place the isolated stone bust onto a different backdrop, such as a library shelf or a digital gallery wall, changing the context without a new photoshoot. You could also separate the bust from its pedestal, moving the subject to a new base or removing it entirely for a floating effect.

**The Honest Edges**
The model works best with sharp, clear images. The fine hair curls will likely have soft, imperfect edges, as photographic cutouts struggle with such detail against a dark background. The inpainted area on the base plate is a plausible recreation, not the original pixels. Each run re-segments the photo, so layer boundaries and names may shift slightly, and a rebuild will be visually close but not pixel-perfect.
