Restore the Photo

A torn, scratched, sepia-faded portrait comes back as a clean print — and an identity guard prints the repair ledger to prove nothing was invented.

Open workflow ↗

Saved run $0.03943 · Live prices vary

How it runs

Restoration is the oldest paid chore in photo editing — human studios charge $20–85 a photo — and its AI failure mode is famous: the tool 'fixes' grandma into a stranger. This workflow is built around that fear. A vision survey inventories the damage before anything is touched; Seedream 5.5 repairs under a hard no-inventions rule; a Pruna upscale returns the print at 2896×2896; then an identity guard compares the result against the original face by face and object by object and prints a repair ledger — repaired, rebuilt, left alone — ending in a HELD or DRIFTED verdict. The demo portrait is a generated 1940s photograph with synthetic damage applied locally (three vertical scratches, one crossing the face; a torn bottom-left corner; a stain; a crease; sepia fade; dust), so the survey and ledger could be graded against known ground truth: the survey found every planted defect and the saved run's ledger keys to it item by item, verdict RESTORE: HELD. The honesty matters because the first probe run failed exactly the way users fear: the restorer invented a necklace the guard initially waved through. The prompts on both sides were hardened (no-additions clause for the restorer, adversarial neck/ears/wrists/background checks for the guard), and the shipped guide keeps the warning in plain sight: the guard is a check, not a guarantee — eyeball jewelry, hands and background yourself. A black-and-white original stays black-and-white; this workflow does not colorize.

Photo to editable layers

One photo of a marble bust returns as four named layers — base plate, background, bust, pedestal — each a transparent PNG with a bounding box; stacked at their coordinates they rebuild the photo (mean abs diff 14.26/255)

Open workflow ↗

Saved run $0.02735 · Live prices vary

How it runs

Layer separation is the oldest ask in photo editing — give me the subject, the background and the props as pieces I can move — and the browser version has always been a mask you paint yourself. This workflow makes it one run: a Gemini survey names what should separate and what will resist, Seedream 5.0 Flash Layerize (bytedance/seedream/v5/flash/layerize, $0.027 flat at every size — the Seedream 5.5 decomposition route bills $0.60) returns a clean base plate plus each element as its own transparent PNG carrying a name, a z-index and a bounding box, and a GLM guide closes with what to do with the stack and the honest edges. The claim was checked by rebuilding: stacking this saved run's background, bust and pedestal at their boxes over the base plate lands at a mean absolute difference of 14.26/255 against the original photo, 82.3% of pixels within 25 — close, visibly the same photograph, not pixel-perfect. The bust cutout is genuinely clean (head, neck and drapery, pedestal excluded, edges tight); the background layer is a soft matte. Honest edges, all measured on this ship's own runs: the model RE-SEGMENTS every run — in the morning probe the bust was z3 and the pedestal z2, in this saved run they are z2 and z3 with reworded names, and recomposition across runs scored 6.65 to 27.30, so treat names and boundaries as per-run, not canonical. Some photos refuse outright with a 422 (the candle product shot from One Photo, Whole Campaign did; refusals are never charged), and identical requests flake — the route went 200/422/200 across this ship's attempts, with remote-URL inputs the flakiest and inlined images landing first try. The base plate behind a lifted element is regenerated inpaint, plausible rather than the original pixels, and fine hair and weathered edges separate worst. Why this shipped the day it did: the model's response always carried all four layers, but every nanoodle executor's edit node kept only the first returned image, so a graph received just the empty base plate — the fix (edit nodes surface every returned image plus the layer metadata) landed across JS, Py, Rust, the editor's embedded engine and nanoodle-mcp on 2026-11-20, and this example is its first proof. The saved run is assembled from two runs of this exact graph and says so in run.json: layers from the run whose layerize call landed, guide text from a second run whose layerize call refused — the guide does not read the layers, only the survey.

Talking photo

A weathered marble emperor, silent for two thousand years, performs one line — frame-checked against the audio: mouth nearly closed at the pause, wide open on the loudest word

Open workflow ↗

Saved run at least $0.725 · Live prices vary

How it runs

Upload one front-facing photo with a story in its face — a statue, a painting, a portrait — write one short line, and the photo performs it. The script node carries a direction line plus the line itself; a Gemini TTS voice (Algenib) performs it, a trim node caps the clip at five seconds, and LTX-2.5 Fast takes the photo and the audio as direct inputs, generated audio off, and moves the mouth to the supplied voice. A GLM guide closes with what you made, how it works, and the honest edges. The demo subject is a cracked marble emperor bust, chipped nose and all, delivering: "Two thousand years silent... and you wake me NOW?" The sync claim was checked in the video, not the listing: a probe run came first, and on this saved run the mouth was frame-checked against the audio — wide open mid-line (1.5s) and closed on the script's ellipsis pause (~3.0s), where the waveform itself goes quiet. The voice pairing was checked the same way: the example first shipped with Sulafat, a female voice (measured 210 Hz) on a marble emperor — caught and called out the same day — and was re-voiced with Algenib, whose performance here measures 143 Hz; the clip above is that corrected run. Two pricing facts the catalog listing does not shout, both measured: audio-driven LTX runs render at 1080p even when 720p is selected, so they bill at the 1080p rate of $0.14 a second, not the $0.10 720p rate — a 5.00s clip bills exactly $0.70 — this saved run billed $0.70, plus about $0.025 of speech and a $0 guide. The honest edges, also printed in the guide: no current model reliably closes the mouth on pauses — in a frame-counted shootout LTX was best of field and still only closed on 2 of 6 pauses — so short lines with few pauses forgive most (this demo's pause did close; do not count on it). Keep scripts near ten words, because every second bills. Front-facing faces with an unobstructed mouth work best, and statues and paintings forgive more than photographs of real people. Have your own voice clip? Replace the speech node with an audio upload wired into the same port.

Mesh diet

The 30,000-triangle dragon from Photo to Game-Ready Mesh, retopologized to a 4,000-face budget by Hyper3D Low Poly — the saved output parses at 3,929 triangles, silhouette intact

Open workflow ↗

Saved run $0.40342 · Live prices vary

How it runs

AI mesh generators hand you a prop with a hundred thousand triangles and call it done; engines budget in thousands. This workflow closes that gap in the browser: Idol Max Turbo rebuilds one photo as a textured mesh of exactly 30,000 triangles, then Hyper3D Low Poly (landed on NanoGPT the day before this shipped) retopologizes it down to a face budget you set — 4,000 here, inside its 200–10,000 range — carrying the UVs and PBR textures over. A Gemini surveyor first writes down the silhouette features that must survive the cut, and the GLM guide closes with the budget table (Roblox caps a mesh at 20,000 triangles; mobile props run 300–3,000; WebGL props 1,000–10,000) and the honest edges. The proof is countable, and the counts are printed: the morning probe parsed at 30,000 in, 3,876 out; this saved run parses at 30,000 in, 3,929 out. Automatic retopology lands NEAR its target, not on it — quote the target as a target. Silhouette was checked by rendering both GLBs from three angles: wings, coiled tail, antlered head and plinth all read at ~4k triangles; the chin whiskers, teeth and wing-tip points merge, exactly what the survey flagged as fragile. Flaws and limits: automatic retopology is not hand retopology — a character that must deform at the joints still wants edge loops a person designs; props, environment pieces and LODs are the claim. The diet is in triangles, not megabytes: the diet GLB is 11.8 MB against the heavy mesh's 10.0 MB, because the transferred textures are as large as the source's — texture resolution is a separate lever. Already have a heavy GLB? Wire it straight into the Low Poly node's model input and skip the rebuild. One pricing note the run exposed: Idol Max Turbo's catalog price is variant-based, and the PBR variant this graph uses bills $0.20, not the $0.10 base. The turntable and the before/after still are plain flat-shaded renders of the saved GLBs with their own base-colour textures, not beauty shots.
