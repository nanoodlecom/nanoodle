Restore the Photo

One damaged photo → a restored print, an identity check, and a repair ledger.

A torn, scratched, sepia-faded portrait comes back as a clean print — and an identity guard prints the repair ledger to prove nothing was invented. · 2026-11-22 · Reported saved run: $0.03943. google/gemini-3.1-flash-lite · bytedance/seedream-v5.5/edit · pruna-ai/p-image/upscale · z-ai/glm-4.6

A Gemini vision survey inventories the damage first — scratches, tears, stains, fading, dust, and what touches each face. Seedream 5.5 repairs it under a hard no-inventions rule, Pruna upscales the result back to print size, and a Gemini identity guard compares the restored print against your original and prints a repair ledger — repaired, rebuilt, left alone — with a RESTORE: HELD or DRIFTED verdict. GLM writes the guide from the survey and the ledger.

References that went in

Restoration is the oldest paid chore in photo editing — human studios charge $20–85 a photo — and its AI failure mode is famous: the tool 'fixes' grandma into a stranger. This workflow is built around that fear. A vision survey inventories the damage before anything is touched; Seedream 5.5 repairs under a hard no-inventions rule; a Pruna upscale returns the print at 2896×2896; then an identity guard compares the result against the original face by face and object by object and prints a repair ledger — repaired, rebuilt, left alone — ending in a HELD or DRIFTED verdict. The demo portrait is a generated 1940s photograph with synthetic damage applied locally (three vertical scratches, one crossing the face; a torn bottom-left corner; a stain; a crease; sepia fade; dust), so the survey and ledger could be graded against known ground truth: the survey found every planted defect and the saved run's ledger keys to it item by item, verdict RESTORE: HELD. The honesty matters because the first probe run failed exactly the way users fear: the restorer invented a necklace the guard initially waved through. The prompts on both sides were hardened (no-additions clause for the restorer, adversarial neck/ears/wrists/background checks for the guard), and the shipped guide keeps the warning in plain sight: the guard is a check, not a guarantee — eyeball jewelry, hands and background yourself. A black-and-white original stays black-and-white; this workflow does not colorize.

Make it yours

  1. Open the graph — save first if the canvas already has your work. Undo brings yours back.
  2. Swap Damaged photo for your own damaged photograph — the worse the damage, the more the survey and ledger matter. Keep Restore — Seedream 5.5 on bytedance/seedream-v5.5/edit and its no-inventions clause intact; it is what stops the restorer adding jewelry, glasses or objects that were never there. The guard's instructions live in its system field and the survey reaches it as its prompt — that wiring is deliberate; do not re-route the survey to the text port.
  3. Check the editor estimate, then run with your NanoGPT key. Four paid calls: the saved run reported $0.0394 in total (Seedream restore $0.028, Pruna upscale $0.01, Gemini survey and guard about $0.0014 together, GLM guide $0.00). Prices and results vary.
  4. Put the restored print beside your original and check the guard's work: every face the same person, the neck, ears and wrists free of invented jewelry, and the ledger's REBUILT list naming anything that had to be reconstructed. On this saved run the ledger keys to the survey item by item and the verdict is RESTORE: HELD.
